When something breaks at home, the story lives in three places

When something in a rented home breaks, the story lives in three places: the tenant's photos, the landlord's inbox and the contractor's phone calls. At move-out, nobody can prove what the place looked like on day one, and a disagreement turns into a dispute.

02

If something goes wrong at home, could you show how it looked before?

Dwelling is photo-first proof of your home, room by room. Onboarding asks one question per screen and adapts to the role, and the rooms step redraws the house as you tap.

Product walkthrough

03

Everyone sees the same truth

Status changes, notifications and the timeline update across the tenant, landlord and contractor views. The language never feels legal: "You see this differently" replaces dispute, and both sides are quoted neutrally. Photos stay central, with dated stamps on your own photos.

04

Move-in against today

The record pays off at the moment it matters: a before and after of the same room, side by side, in three modes.

05

The audit, and what it fixed

Start over now confirms instead of wiping everything. A report can be undone or withdrawn until the landlord has seen it. Primary buttons moved from 2.73:1 contrast to 4.8:1, and across 106 screen states there are no controls under 44 px and no text under 12 px.

06

What came out of it

The audit score moved from 6.8 to 7.5 to 8.1 out of 10 across three rounds. Across 106 screen states there are zero controls under 44 px, zero unlabeled controls, no text under 12 px and no contrast failures.

Help and documentation went from 4 to 8 with a searchable "How Dwelling works", and user control from 5.5 to 8.5.

07

Looking back

Six stock room photos, a single HTML file and no server, so accounts, invites and notifications live in the browser. It forced me to make the experience carry itself with no backend.

I'd give it a real backend sooner so invites and notifications sync between phones. A shared record needs a shared source.

A neutral voice does as much for trust as any feature. When the words stop sounding legal, people stop sounding defensive.
