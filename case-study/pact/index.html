01

Group money runs on one person and a group chat

In Nigeria, money for a birthday gift, a trip or an owambe moves through one friend's bank account while the status lives in a WhatsApp thread. Nobody knows the real total. One person ends up chasing everyone, and trust carries the whole thing.

Each failure became a principle, and each principle became something on the screen.

  • “What should we get Kemi?”
  • “I'll send mine on Friday”
ProblemPrincipleResponse
Nobody knows the real totalShow the money, all of it, to everyoneA ring made of each person's colour, and a record of every contribution
One person ends up chasing everyoneLet the product do the chasingPledges with a reminder on the day, and reminders three days before the deadline
Trust carries the whole thingAgree the rules before any money movesA missed-goal rule, refund everyone or keep what was raised, which runs on its own
The money leaves through one accountSpend in the open, with a second pair of eyesVendors paid from the Pact, each payment on the record, a co-organiser's approval past 200,000 naira a day
02

Five things a group does together

Saving toward a goal is one moment in a group's life. Before it, someone asks who is free. After it, someone paid for dinner and wants it settled.

Pact

A shared goal with money behind it: a target, a deadline and a rule for a missed goal.

Plan

Something happening on a date. Who is in, and who is doing what.

Ask

A question to the group. Pick one of a list, or say whether you are in.

Split

Something already paid for. Pact keeps the record of who owes what.

Circle

The people you make things happen with, and everything you do together.

03

One screen to answer how far along we are

The Pact screen has one job: let anyone in the group see, in a second, how much has come in, from whom, and what is left.

  1. Faces before figures. The people in the Pact come first, with one line on how they are doing as a group.
  2. A ring of everyone's colours. Each person who puts in adds a segment in their own colour. Tap one to see who gave it.
  3. Numbers are the biggest thing. People, what is left and the deadline, in one line, never wrapped.
  4. One solid action. Add money is the only dark pill on the screen.
  5. The second action is quieter. Invite people sits in the Pact's own tint.
04

Putting money in

Money only moves with a deliberate act. You hold the button, then confirm with a four-digit PIN.

  • Amounts come as quick picks.
  • A single tap covers whatever is left.
  • A contribution never overshoots the goal.
05

What came out of it

Pact went from a first sketch to a working product in one month, 25 September to 26 October 2026, with 147 commits. It runs end to end in a sandbox beta: Home, Pacts, Plans, Asks, Splits, Circles, contributions, reminders and payouts, plus the marketing site and install page.

The numbers I will watch once the beta opens are time to first contribution, share of pledges kept and how often a group finishes a Pact.

06

Looking back

One person, one month, and a real payments rail to design around. That forced a small, opinionated set of objects instead of a flexible toolkit, and a sandbox with test payments before any real money moves.

I'd put the first Pact in front of a real group in week one, not week four. The flows are right on paper, and a group chat will tell me which reminder wording actually gets people to pay.

Make the money visible and the chasing disappears. Agreeing the rules before any money moves removes most arguments later. And owning strategy, design and build means every trade-off is a decision I can make in an afternoon.
