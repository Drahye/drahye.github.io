Kivo

Learning that measures what people can actually do.

01

Courses track completion, learners need to know what they can do

Courses track completion. Learners finish them and still cannot answer "can I actually do this?" Completion is not mastery, and certificates do not tell a hiring manager, or the learner, what someone can do.

02

Start with the outcome you want

Kivo organises learning around capabilities and evidence instead of lessons. Onboarding starts from the outcome, asks for a time budget, then runs a few short checks with no hints and no pressure to find a starting point.

Walkthrough

Product walkthrough

03

Every state is earned

Capability state is derived, never typed in. Confidence is tracked separately and shown as a quadrant, the quickest way to find false confidence. Decay never punishes: "Needs refresh" and a five-minute check replace "lost" or "expired".

04

Proof, mapped to capabilities

Evidence is anything that shows you can do something: a challenge, a project, a work sample. Projects map to capabilities, and readiness is explainable down to the point. Copy never promises hiring outcomes.

  • Verify mode has no hints: asking for help turns the attempt into practice.
  • Mentors verify evidence and give feedback tied to a capability.
  • Sync is offline-first, so a mentor review is never overwritten by a stale device.
  • AI is optional, labelled, and never touches evidence, mastery or readiness.
05

What came out of it

Kivo runs as an installable PWA with a Node server for accounts, sync, mentor links, uploads and goal authoring. Five goals ship ready: Junior Data Analyst, Stronger Product Designer, Confident Public Speaker, Conversational French and Emerging Leader, and learners can build their own.

A planner decides what to work on next from weight, prerequisites and what is overdue, so the Today screen always has one honest answer.

06

Looking back

A two-sided product with a single builder. The mentor side had to exist for the learner side to mean anything, so both shipped together, sharing one engine.

I'd put the Confidence by Evidence quadrant in front of real learners first. It is the screen I'm proudest of, and the one most worth watching someone read.

Evidence beats progress bars. When every state has to be earned, the interface stops flattering people and starts helping them.
