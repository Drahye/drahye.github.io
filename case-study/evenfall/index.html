01

A busy business still does not know what it can spend

A Lagos food business takes money through a POS terminal, bank transfers, delivery platforms and cash in a till. Settlements land late or short, fees come out unannounced, payroll is due on Friday, and the owner's bank balance says nothing about which of those naira are already spoken for.

  • “Till is 5,500 short”
  • “Missing settlement, 40,000”
  • “Payroll is on Friday”
02

Safe to use, and what it leaves out on purpose

Everything on Today hangs from one figure: what the business is safe to use right now. It is the settled money in connected accounts, minus protected money such as payroll and a minimum reserve, minus unpaid bills due in the next 48 hours.

Expected money never counts until it lands. Payroll counts at its full requirement even when only part of it is set aside, so funding the gap makes the protection real instead of moving the number.

03

Open, reconcile, close

Most finance tools are a running ledger with no edges. Evenfall gives the day a start and an end. Morning Open records an opening position that does not move when live numbers do. Close the day walks through the totals, the till count and every open exception, resolved or carried forward on purpose, before the day is sealed.

04

Every mismatch leads to an action

Reconcile lists what does not match yet, ordered by urgency: an unusual refund pattern, a missing settlement, a shortfall, a possible duplicate payment, an unmatched bank credit. Each exception type has its own actions, and a gap explained down to zero resolves itself.

05

On a phone, the day is for decisions

06

A forecast built only from dated money, and roles for everyone

Forecast is built from settlements, invoices, bills and repeating payments. It draws a cautious line that counts expected money at 60 per cent, and one for if expected money arrives. What-if levers never touch real records.

Five roles share one workspace. Operations staff never see balances, a bookkeeper cannot move money or close the day, and payments over a limit by anyone but the owner become approval requests.

07

What came out of it

Evenfall is a responsive web app with roles, onboarding and a financial engine with no screen code inside it. The engine passes 27 checks, and 44 browser tests cover flows, keyboard use and accessibility.

The business, Ade Foods, shows 2,729,000 naira safe to use, payroll protection for Friday and trading so far by channel, all on one Today screen.

08

Looking back

Real finance logic with simulated data. I wrote the engine first, with no UI inside it, so every number on screen could be traced and tested before any design depended on it.

I'd sit with a restaurant owner during a close. The 60 per cent weighting on expected money is a defensible default, and I'd like it to come from someone counting a till at 11 PM.

A trustworthy number needs its exclusions shown as clearly as its inclusions. Giving the day a start and an end did more for calm than any dashboard could.
