01

What happens when the person who knows cannot answer?

Most of a life runs on knowledge that sits in one head: which account pays the rent, who holds the spare key, how Mum's support is sent, what the dog takes at 8 PM. If the person is in hospital, travelling or unreachable, someone else rebuilds their life from texts, drawers and guesses.

LastKnown is not a will builder, a password manager or a document vault. It stores who knows and where, never the secret itself.

  • “I send Mum $500 every Friday”
  • “Bruno's medication, 8 PM”
  • “Rent goes out on the 1st”
02

An emergency tool which must not feel like preparing for death

The product has two moments with opposite moods. In everyday use it has to feel like caring for the continuity of your life. In an emergency it has to give someone calm, specific instructions. Most tools in this space lean on fear, legal language or an archive, and people avoid them.

Responsibilities over documents

A file supports a responsibility. It is never the structure.

Action over archive

The person who steps in sees what to do first.

Plain language

"Needs a backup" and "only you know this", never "critical failure".

Progressive

Nobody has to finish setup in one sitting.

03

From a vault to a map of what leans on you

The first idea was an encrypted folder. The refined idea moved the core object from the document to the responsibility, and the main question from "where is it stored?" to "who would need this, and when?"

People who know a responsibility appear as small avatars on its row. If nobody does, an empty dashed circle says so.

04

One sentence becomes structure

Capture has to feel like telling a friend. Quick Add turns "I send Mum $500 every Friday from my RBC account" into an amount, a weekly schedule, a method and a source, then asks you to confirm. What breaks first lets you pick how long you might be away and reorders responsibilities by when someone would need to act.

05

Preview before you share

Trust comes from seeing the result. "This is exactly what Sarah will see" renders the same data a trusted person would get. Access opens under five conditions written as a person would say them: always, emergency only, after inactivity, when I release it, after verified death.

06

What came out of it

LastKnown is a working app with a web companion, 16 designed screens, a UX review and a UI audit behind it. Warm for everyday use, dark and action-first for the person who steps in.

07

Looking back

A subject nobody wants to open. The constraint was tone: no fear, no legal language, no pressure to finish. Everything had to be possible a little at a time.

I'd test the emergency view with someone who has actually had to step into another person's life. How they read the first screen matters more than anything I can reason out.

Calm is a design decision you make in the words. "Not yet" instead of a countdown, and a count with a reason instead of a locked title, did more than any visual treatment.
