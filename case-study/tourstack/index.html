01

Two products for the people who make live shows

Tourstack is Ckrowd's platform for the entertainment industry: live shows, events and the people who work on them. Ckrowd itself is a creator-economy platform, and I have led product design there since November 2024.

This page follows two pieces of that work, both designed and built by me. One is a talent platform where creative people find paid work and take courses. The other is the buying experience for a live tour.

Talent dashboard

Gigs, learning and reputation in one product. 271 rules, built from 18 August to 14 September 2026.

Campus Takeover tickets

Tickets for a three city tour, one per student, each with its own QR code. Built from 30 September 2026.

02

Work, learning and reputation in one account

The talent dashboard serves creative talent in music, film and the wider creative industries. Talent find paid work, grow a network, publish a portfolio and take structured courses in one account, in English or French.

I worked on it alone. I defined the modules and the sections inside each one, turned the scope into a requirements document, and built it. The document lists eight modules and 271 numbered rules. Learn holds 45 of them, more than any other module. Gigs holds 31.

The platform has no launch date yet, so this page describes what shipped in the build, not how it performed.

  • Home: stats, recommended gigs, continue learning and announcements.
  • Gigs: search, match scores with reasons, applications with a timeline.
  • Learn: a catalogue, lessons, quizzes and signed certificates.
  • Portfolio, Network, Community, Messages and Marketplace around them.
03

From a posted gig to a portfolio credit

Gigs has two sides. A client posts work and talent apply. The flow carries both sides through to a review and a credit on the talent's portfolio.

Each open gig gets a score from 0 to 100 for each talent, with the two strongest reasons shown. The weights sit in one config file, so tuning them needs no change to the scoring code.

  1. Post. A client posts a gig in a four step form: the work, the place, the budget and a preview.
  2. Match. Every talent sees a score and the two signals that contribute most.
  3. Apply. A cover note, up to five portfolio items, a proposed rate and confirmed availability.
  4. Shortlist. Shortlisting opens a conversation in Messages and releases the applicant's contact details.
  5. Credit. Both sides rate the gig, and one step adds it to the portfolio as an unverified credit.

04

Decisions about money, trust and contact

Four rules shaped how the product behaves, and each one is written into the requirements document.

Gig payments stay off platform

The platform records rates and agreements and moves no money for gigs. Clients and talent settle fees between themselves.

One application per gig

The database blocks a second application. The apply panel shows the existing application's status instead.

Closed gigs stay visible

A filled, completed or cancelled gig stays open to read, with the apply action off and the reason shown.

Contact details after shortlist

Clients see full portfolios from the start, and private contact details only after shortlisting.

05

Learning that counts toward the work

Learn is a learning management system, not a video list. It carries a catalogue, lessons, quizzes, assignments, live sessions and signed certificates.

A certificate earned in Learn appears on the talent's portfolio and counts toward the gig match score. A certificate page confirms what Ckrowd issued and whether the record still matches it. It is not an assessment of the holder by anybody else.

06

Three cities, one artist, a ticket for every student

Sanlam Allianz is taking Ayo Maff and friends on a Campus Takeover: one show each at the University of Ibadan, Obafemi Awolowo University in Ile-Ife and the Olusegun Obasanjo Presidential Library Ground in Abeokuta. Tourstack sells the tickets, so I designed and built the ticket buying experience.

The buyers are students on their phones, often buying for friends too. Every ticket comes with Sanlam Allianz insurance, so each guest registers their own details and their cover is linked to them.

Student ticketing usually fails in the same places: a fee appears at the last step, a friend's ticket ends up on your phone, a card payment fails and the form starts again. The landing page makes four promises against those, and the flow keeps each one.

PromiseWhat the flow does
The full price upfrontFees sit in the order summary from the first ticket, beside every step of checkout.
A ticket in your pocketA QR code per ticket, on the phone, with download, print and add to calendar.
Everyone gets their ownEach ticket asks for its holder's details and is issued in their name, with its own QR code.
Pay your wayCard, bank transfer or USSD. A failed payment keeps every answer.
07

Pick a city first, because each one sells its own tickets

The same artist plays three grounds on three dates, and each city has its own tickets. So the event page asks for the city before anything else. Changing city starts the selection again, so nobody pays for Ibadan with an Abeokuta basket.

Each city also has a page of its own. A campus ambassador in Ife shares a link to Ife only: its venue, its date and its prices, with checkout locked to it.

Prices on these screens are placeholders from the proposal deck. Dates, prices and fees were still to be confirmed when they were captured.

08

One question at a time, for every ticket

Three tickets means three people, and the organiser needs each one on record. A long form for three people on a phone is where buyers give up. So checkout asks one short part at a time, labels whose ticket it is for, and shows progress across all of them: part 1 of 10 for three tickets.

Every question, its type, its help text and the rule deciding when it shows is defined in one config file. Adding or changing a question is a change to data, not to a screen.

Answers stay with their ticket

Change the quantities mid-checkout and each holder's answers stay with the ticket type they belong to.

No two holders alike

An email or mobile number already used for another ticket is stopped on the spot, with the ticket it clashes with named.

Asked once per order

The referral code and consent come once, at the end, not once per ticket.

Nothing lost on the way

Answers survive going Back and a failed payment.

09

No surprises between the basket and the ticket

Review shows every ticket with its holder, and the total with its fees already in it. Payment offers card, bank transfer and USSD. The confirmation is the ticket itself, not a page about it.

The duplicate guard names the ticket an email clashes with and says why it matters, and the confirmation email carries every ticket in the order.

10

Built in a month, ready for its backend

The first version went up on 30 September 2026 and the page per city on 30 October. It runs on mock data and a mock payment provider, with Paystack and Flutterwave ready behind the same interface. Swapping the mocks for the real backend is a change of adapter bodies, not of screens.

The version shown here runs on mock data, so this page holds no sales numbers. With real sales, I will watch where people leave checkout, and how often the duplicate guard fires on a first try.
