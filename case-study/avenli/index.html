01

Health history lives in scattered notes and memory

Someone with migraines walks into an appointment and is asked how often, how bad, since when, and whether the new medication helped. The answer is somewhere in notes apps, photos, a calendar and memory, and it comes out as "a few times, I think it is better".

  • “Headache again, left side”
  • “Slept 5h 40m”
  • “Started Propranolol 40 mg”
02

One Story follows one thing over time

Everything belongs to a Story, and a Story tracks only what you chose for it. Logging takes seconds: three taps for a symptom with defaults, time set to now, severity and duration shown only if the Story tracks them.

The severity slider starts unset instead of at 5, because a starting value anchors ratings.

03

A change in the line sits next to a change in life

A Story opens with a plain summary, then What's changed: every entry as a dot, height for severity, size for duration. Turn on the medication overlay and the day Propranolol started shades the rest of the chart.

Insights only say what your own logs show and how many entries they rest on: six entries in the last four weeks against seven before, average severity down from 6.0 to 3.8. Every insight carries a caveat.

04

Walk into the appointment prepared

Prepare turns months of history into the few things worth discussing: the last 30 days in four figures, the chart, the medication and its start date, what the logs show, and the questions saved for the appointment. It shares, copies or prints to a single page, always in the light theme.

05

Careful by design

Avenli does not diagnose or give medical advice, and it never recommends or changes a dose. Urgent words typed anywhere bring up the emergency message without blocking the entry. Features that are not built are not shown.

A UX and UI audit closed with its findings fixed: undo replaced "are you sure?" for single deletions, hidden layers stopped catching keyboard focus, the text floor rose to 12 pixels, and a dark theme arrived with no text under 4.5 to 1.

06

What came out of it

In the Migraine Story, six entries in the last four weeks against seven before, and average severity down from 6.0 to 3.8, now read as a single page.

It is an installable web app that works offline with data kept on the device. 07

Looking back

Health data is sensitive, so everything stays on the device: no accounts, no server. That shaped the whole product toward being useful alone, offline, and in a waiting room.

I'd run the brief past a clinician early. I wrote it for the patient, and a doctor's three-second read of it is the real test.

Restraint is the feature. Saying what the data shows, with the count and a caveat, earns more trust than any insight pretending to know why.
