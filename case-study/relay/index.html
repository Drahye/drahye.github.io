Giving a workplace a memory that outlasts the shift.

01

A building forgets every eight hours

Hotels run all day and all night. Three shifts take turns in the same building, and the people who start a shift did not see what happened in the last one, while the people who finished it are asleep.

What they knew survives through verbal handovers, WhatsApp threads, paper logbooks, whiteboards and memory. A note like "dryer broken, told eng" exists, and the next person still has to find out which dryer, whether it is still broken, who has it, and whether it happened before.

  • “Dryer three started vibrating badly”
  • “Guest in 305 asked for extra towels”
02

Eight questions every update should answer

My first framing was the obvious one: workers need a better digital shift report. I sketched it, and the sketch showed the flaw. A tidier logbook still answers only the first of the questions the next shift has.

The reframe: people do not need more notes, they need operational information with context. So Relay organises around the workplace itself, its spaces, rooms and equipment, instead of around people, messages or channels.

  • What happened, and where?
  • To what, or to whom?
  • Does it still matter?
  • Who owns the next step?
  • Has the next team seen it?

03

Object-centred memory won over three other models

I explored four models. A digital notebook was WhatsApp with a nicer skin. An operations inbox kept urgency but lost the building. A spatial map was heavy to set up. The fourth attached every update to the room, space or machine it concerns.

Information also ages at different speeds, so every update gets one of three lifespans in plain words: expires after this shift, stays until resolved, saved to history.

04

Since you left, as a briefing worked down a stack

Relay opens a shift with "Good morning, Amaka. 15 things changed while you were away." Each issue shows its plate, the problem, the owner and the next step. Sheets you have been through file themselves above as struck-through plates, and the briefing ends with "You're caught up." and one action, Start shift, which records the acknowledgement.

05

Capture, resolve, hand over

Capture ties a note to the room or machine it concerns. Resolve closes it so the next shift sees it closed. End shift only passes when nothing is left unowned.

06

What came out of it

Relay is a working Next.js app covering the briefing, capture, object memory, handoff and Walk the Space, backed by unit tests and Playwright flows. It ships with a design system of its own: tokens, type, a motion system and a dark theme for night work.

Behind it sit desk research, a research plan, ten hypotheses, a risk register, four concepts and four wireframe rounds.

07

Looking back

A fictional hotel, two days and a blank repository. I invented the operation in enough detail, rooms, equipment, five roles and three shifts, that every screen had to answer a real question from someone on a real shift.

I'd shadow a night manager before drawing anything. The eight questions came from reasoning about handovers, and I'd rather hear them from the person holding the logbook.

People say "the dryer", not "the message from 23:35". Anchoring information to the thing it concerns changed the whole product. A better report was the wrong problem, and the sketch is what told me.
